<section className="editorial-section editorial-section--research">
  <div className="editorial-section__intro flow">
    <p className="editorial-eyebrow">04. Outcomes</p>
    <h2 className="type-title">SIT/UAT Improvement: reached > 99%</h2>
    <p>Pushing for a bigger test coverage, despite the bigger initial work load, was the better idea because it helped to raise bugs earlier on, rather than leaving it till deployment to be found. this was evident from the 1% failed test case during UAT, where there was a system limitation raised and needed external expertise for a follow up on the case </p>

    <p>Regression testing was especially important as the recruiting workflow reached 20 different agencies. Each of this had their own localised flow that differed, such as where the generation of documents occured, where the asssement takes place etc. More than just ensuring the new feature worked, it was even more important to ensure that existing workflows for ineligible cases do not get impacted negatively. </p>

  </div>
  
</section>

<section className="editorial-section editorial-section--outcome">
  <div className="editorial-section__intro editorial-section__intro--storyboards flow">
    <p className="editorial-eyebrow">5. Takeaways</p>
    <h2 className="type-title">Designing for data</h2>
    <p>Across my enhancements, my contribution often involved connecting a user-facing requirement to the underlying workflows, organisational rules, and testing needed to support it. The reference-check work particularly challenged me to take ownership of an incomplete handover, navigate stakeholder communications and collaborate closely with the client team under tight capacity constraints.</p>

  </div>
  <div className="editorial-section__intro editorial-section__intro--storyboards flow">
    <h2 className="type-title">What I took away.</h2>
    <p>In academic projects, I often had the freedom to take an idea from zero to one. In enterprise work, I learned to start from what already existed: inherited decisions, policy constraints and live data.</p>
    <p>One limitation of this work was that I could not conduct direct user research with agency users. The pain points and requirements came through the clientele, which meant I had to distinguish stakeholder-reported needs from behaviour I had personally observed. Given another iteration, I would prioritise direct observation and usability validation with the people carrying out these tasks to get a first-person validation of the user experience.</p>
    <blockquote className="editorial-quote">
      <em>
        Good enterprise product design is not just what users see. It is how
        clearly the data, rules and exceptions behind that experience are
        designed for the next person who has to maintain them.
      </em>
    </blockquote>
  </div>
</section>
