<Statement label="The challenge" tone="light">

How could I make it easier for customers to find a BBQ restaurant, build an order, and check out within a single app?

</Statement>

<section className="editorial-section editorial-section--overview">
  <div className="editorial-section__intro">
    <p className="editorial-eyebrow">01. Overview</p>
    <h2 className="type-title">A mobile ordering experience designed for BBQ lovers.</h2>
    <p>BarbeQueue was a mobile ordering concept designed as a solo UX course project. The idea was to bring BBQ restaurant discovery and ordering into one place, supporting dine-in, pre-queue, and catering orders.</p>
    <p>I explored the initial experience through wireframing, prototyping, usability testing, and one round of design revisions.</p>
  </div>
  <div className="editorial-section__visual">
    <Figure
      src="/images/work/2023-05-barbequeue-google-ux-certificate/assets/KeyScreens.png"

      aspect="16/9"
    />
  </div>
</section>

<section className="editorial-section editorial-section--problem">
  <div className="editorial-section__intro">
    <p className="editorial-eyebrow">02. The problem</p>
    <h2 className="type-title">Three friction points in the ordering experience.</h2>
    <p>User research and usability testing highlighted three opportunities to make ordering more convenient.</p>
    <blockquote className="editorial-quote">“This is annoying, I'm not here just to order 1 satay only.”</blockquote>
  </div>
  <div className="problem-cards">
    <article className="problem-card">
      <span className="problem-card__number">01</span>
      <h3 className="type-subtitle">Ordering multiple items was frustrating.</h3>
      <p>Participants struggled to build a complete meal when the flow focused on individual selections. 4 of 7 participants wanted to order multiple menu items, and 3 of them also wanted a cart.</p>
    </article>
    <article className="problem-card">
      <span className="problem-card__number">02</span>
      <h3 className="type-subtitle">Returning customers wanted a faster route to ordering.</h3>
      <p>Repeat customers wanted to reach familiar restaurants without starting from discovery each time. 5 of 7 participants prioritised completing an order in under five minutes, while 4 wanted to favourite restaurants.</p>
    </article>
    <article className="problem-card">
      <span className="problem-card__number">03</span>
      <h3 className="type-subtitle">Checkout needed more payment flexibility.</h3>
      <p>Participants expected cash on delivery to be available as a way to complete their order. 6 of 7 participants wanted that option.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section className="editorial-section editorial-section--outcome">
  <div className="editorial-section__intro">
    <p className="editorial-eyebrow">04. Outcome</p>
    <h2 className="type-title">A more flexible ordering experience.</h2>
    <p>The revised concept responded to the usability feedback by introducing a multi-item cart, saved restaurants, and another payment option.</p>
    <p className="type-small editorial-section__note">The project concluded after one usability study and one round of revisions. I did not conduct a second test, so these improvements were not verified through follow-up testing.</p>
  </div>
  <ul className="outcome-list">
    <li><span>01</span><strong>Easier multi-item ordering</strong></li>
    <li><span>02</span><strong>Faster access to favourite restaurants</strong></li>
    <li><span>03</span><strong>More flexible payment choices</strong></li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section className="editorial-section editorial-section--reflection">
  <div className="editorial-section__intro">
    <p className="editorial-eyebrow">05. Reflection</p>
    <h2 className="type-title">What I took away.</h2>
    <p>This project gave me experience in taking a mobile app concept through research, prototyping, usability testing, and design iteration. It also showed me how individual usability issues can influence the overall ordering experience.</p>
    <p>If I continued the project, I would test the revised multi-item ordering flow and revisit the distinction between “Catering” and “Dine-In”, which also caused confusion during the first study.</p>
  </div>
  <ol className="takeaway-list">
    <li><span>01</span><strong>Design around actual user behaviour.</strong></li>
    <li><span>02</span><strong>Use testing to challenge initial assumptions.</strong></li>
    <li><span>03</span><strong>Consider different needs and preferences in checkout design.</strong></li>
  </ol>
</section>
