</Statement>

<section className="editorial-section editorial-section--problem">
  <div className="editorial-section__intro flow">
    <p className="editorial-eyebrow">04. Findings</p>
    <h2 className="type-title">Three recurring needs shaped the next version.</h2>
    <p>The sessions revealed three themes across the ordering journey: people wanted to order quickly, customise a complete meal, and choose how they paid.</p>
    <blockquote className="editorial-quote">“This is annoying, I'm not here just to order 1 satay only.”</blockquote>
  </div>
  <div className="problem-cards">
    <article className="card problem-card flow">
      <span className="problem-card__number">01</span>
      <h3 className="type-subtitle">People wanted to order quickly.</h3>
      <p>5 of 7 participants wanted to order from their chosen restaurant in under five minutes. 4 also wanted to favourite their go-to restaurants.</p>
    </article>
    <article className="card problem-card flow">
      <span className="problem-card__number">02</span>
      <h3 className="type-subtitle">People wanted more customisation.</h3>
      <p>4 of 7 participants wanted to order more than one menu option, and 3 of them wanted a cart to save their selections.</p>
    </article>
    <article className="card problem-card flow">
      <span className="problem-card__number">03</span>
      <h3 className="type-subtitle">People wanted payment-on-delivery.</h3>
      <p>6 of 7 participants wanted cash on delivery, so checkout could not assume that online payment was the only route.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section className="editorial-section editorial-section--requirements">

    <h2 className="type-title">Each research theme became a design requirement.</h2>
    <p>The research did not point toward adding features for their own sake. It showed which parts of the journey needed to change and what the next version had to support.</p>
  </div>
  <ul className="numbered-list requirements-list">

    <li><span>02</span><strong>An updatable cart for multiple menu items</strong></li>
    <li><span>03</span><strong>Clearer item selection and customisation</strong></li>
    <li><span>04</span><strong>A cash-on-delivery option at checkout</strong></li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section className="editorial-section editorial-section--reflection">
  <div className="editorial-section__intro flow">
    <p className="editorial-eyebrow">07. Reflection</p>
    <h2 className="type-title">What I took away.</h2>
    <p>This project was my first experience taking a mobile app concept through research, prototyping, usability testing, and design iteration. It showed me how a confusing label, a hard-to-find action, and the absence of a cart could all point to one larger issue: the app was not following the order people expected.</p>
    <p>If I continued the project, I would test the revised multi-item ordering flow and revisit the distinction between “Catering” and “Dine-In”, which also caused confusion during the first study.</p>
  </div>
  <ol className="numbered-list">
    <li><span>01</span><strong>Design around actual user behaviour.</strong></li>
    <li><span>02</span><strong>Use testing to challenge initial assumptions.</strong></li>
    <li><span>03</span><strong>Consider different needs and preferences in checkout design.</strong></li>
  </ol>
</section>
